Why Equipment Is Abandoned

Main Factors

  • Dust — Covers solar panels, reduces power. Fine abrasive particles (InSight, Spirit).
  • Extreme cold — Lunar nights ~-170°C freeze lubricants and batteries (Surveyor, Apollo).
  • Mechanical failure — Wheel motors seize, soft low-gravity sand traps rovers (Spirit at Troy).
  • Power loss — No reliable dust cleaning; solar output falls below survival threshold.
  • No recovery — Once power dies, communication ends permanently.

How to Prevent

  • Active dust removal: electrodynamic shields, robotic arm sand trickling (InSight technique).
  • Use RTG power instead of solar for multi-year missions.
  • Improved mobility: lower ground pressure wheels, better sand traction models.
  • Cold-tolerant materials, survival heaters, magnetic bearings.
  • Higher redundancy: dual systems, robust hibernation modes.
  • Test in low-gravity sand simulators before launch.

Equipment Catalog

Spirit

14.57°S, 175.47°E — Troy, Gusev Crater.
Stuck 2009, silent 2010. Dragged wheel found silica (ancient water).
Remains: panels, cameras, silicon disk with millions of names.

InSight

4.50°N, 135.62°E — Elysium Planitia.
Retired 2022 from dust. Detected marsquakes. Still visible from orbit.
Remains: SEIS, HP3, arm, dust-covered panels.

Surveyor 3

Ocean of Storms (Apollo 12 site).
Survived lunar nights. Visited by astronauts 1969. Camera returned to Earth.
Remains: structure and scoop.
